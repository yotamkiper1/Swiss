Collect

Found something worth keeping?

Drop, paste or drag it into a project: a link, a picture from a web page, a file, a quick note. If you turn on Recent, what you copy and every screenshot you take wait there for a few days. One tap keeps an item in a project.

  • Off until you turn it on. Capture of the clipboard, screenshots and downloads starts only after you switch it on in Settings ▸ Capture.
  • Never your passwords. Items that apps mark as private are skipped, and password managers are excluded.
  • Quick save from any app. ⌥⌘V saves what you just copied.
Organize

A little order. In one click.

Every project is a tab. Inside, folders made for each kind of thing: Linker for links, PhotoBook for pictures, Notepad for notes, Files for the rest. Add tags, drag things between folders, and copy whole folders from one project to another.

A board full of loose things? Press the wand and they move into folders by kind: links into Linker, pictures into PhotoBook, notes into Notepad, everything else into Files. If some of those folders already exist, Swiss asks whether to add to them.

The wand sorts by kind (links, pictures, notes, files), not by topic. It works in the folder you're in, or on the whole board.

Connected folders

Your folders. A faster way in.

Connect any folder from Finder and it shows up in Swiss's sidebar, next to your projects. Nothing is copied. Swiss works on the folder itself.

  • Changes are real. Rename, move, tag or delete in Swiss and the same happens in Finder.
  • And the other way round. Changes made in Finder show up in Swiss.
  • Sorted your way. Arrange by name, kind, date or tags.
The Frame

Keep your references beside your work.

A canvas that floats above every app. Pull in the pictures, notes and links you need, play a tutorial, and keep working in the app underneath.

  1. Pull from your library

    The library beside the canvas lists your projects and Recent. Drag a reference in, or double-click to add it.

  2. Watch while you work

    YouTube links and video files play right on the canvas, above the app you're working in.

  3. Arrange freely

    Zoom, pan, and move things around. Made a mistake? ⌘Z.

Fits your workspace

Just the space you need.

Start small from the menu bar and step up when you need room. The green button steps up a size, the orange one steps back down.

  • Pop-up

    Drops down from the menu bar, or opens anywhere with ⌥ Space.

  • Window

    A regular window with the sidebar, projects and connected folders.

  • Full screen

    All the room there is, for sorting a big project.

The small blades

Useful details.

Convert

Right-click ▸ Convert. Pictures to JPG, PNG, HEIC, TIFF or PDF, or smaller. Videos to MP4, MOV, M4V, a smaller size, or sound only. Documents to PDF. Videos and YouTube links to MP3 or MP4.

Share

Right-click anything to share it with AirDrop, Mail, Messages and more, including WhatsApp.

Undo and recover

Deleted things stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days. Saves are checked before they replace the last copy, and hourly backups are kept.

Touch ID lock

Optional, in Settings ▸ Privacy. Swiss locks again when your Mac locks or sleeps.

Big files stay put

Files over 250 MB (you can change the limit) are linked rather than copied, so your library doesn't fill your disk.

Press Space on anything to preview it, like in Finder.

Private by design

Everything stays on your Mac.

No account, no servers, no analytics. Swiss only goes online for things you start: fetching a saved link's title and picture, a picture you drag in from a web page, a video you play, a download you ask for.

  • Your library is ordinary files. Pictures, notes and links are saved as real files in a hidden folder in Documents, so they take disk space like any file. If iCloud syncs your Documents folder, it syncs these too.
  • Careful with files. Files that can run code ask before they open, and downloads are marked like Safari's.

Build it on your Mac

Swiss isn't signed and notarized by Apple yet, so there's no download here for now. Its source is public, and you can build it in a couple of minutes.

  1. Install Xcode from the App Store (Swift 5.10 or later), and open it once.
  2. In Terminal, get the source: 
    git clone https://github.com/yotamkiper1/Swiss.git
cd Swiss
  3. Build and install it: 
    ./build.sh install
    This puts Swiss in Applications and opens it. Look for the knife in your menu bar.

FAQ

Questions.

What can I keep in Swiss?

Links, pictures, files, notes and folders. Drop, paste or drag them in from any app. YouTube links and video files can also play on the Frame.

Does clipboard capture happen automatically?

No. Capture of the clipboard, screenshots and downloads is off until you turn it on (Settings ▸ Capture, or the switches at the top of Recent). Once it's on, new items wait in Recent for 3 days by default, and you keep the ones you want. Passwords and items apps mark as private are never captured.

Where are my files stored?

On your Mac, as ordinary files and folders in a hidden folder inside Documents (~/Documents/.Stash), so they use disk space like any other file. Very large files are linked instead of copied. Swiss has no cloud of its own; if iCloud syncs your Documents folder, it syncs this folder too.

How do connected folders work?

You pick a folder in Finder and Swiss shows it in the sidebar. Renaming, moving, tagging and deleting in Swiss change the real folder, and changes made in Finder show up in Swiss. Nothing is copied.

Can I watch a tutorial while working?

Yes. Put a YouTube link or a video file on the Frame. It floats above your other apps, so the video stays in view while you work underneath.

Which Macs and macOS versions are supported?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Macs with Apple silicon. Intel Macs aren't supported yet.

How do I install Swiss?

Until a signed download is ready, you build it from the source with Xcode: three commands, listed in Get Swiss. When a release is published on GitHub, the download appears there automatically.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-in and no server. Swiss only goes online for things you start, like fetching a saved link's title.

How do I report a problem or send an idea?

In Swiss, choose Help ▸ Report a Problem or Send Feedback. It opens an email you read and send yourself. Or write to kipery20@gmail.com.
